How the Pre-Monsoon Readiness Score Works

Three-Mechanism Scoring Model

PMRS = 0.45 × Pluvial + 0.30 × Fluvial + 0.25 × Compound

Grounded in IIT Delhi Drainage Master Plan for NCT Delhi (2018) — SWMM-based simulation of 3 major basins (Najafgarh, Barapullah, Trans-Yamuna) with junction-flooding as the primary failure metric. Separates three distinct flood mechanisms that the 2023 Delhi event proved must be modelled independently.

Mechanism 1 — Pluvial (45%)

Pluvial = MEAN(min(100, Ceff / (Rpeak × Trunoff) × 18.5)) + terrain_adj − infra_penalty
Rain-on-drain flooding

Rainfall exceeds drain conveyance capacity. IIT Delhi DMP found 3,854 flooded junctions in Najafgarh basin alone under a 2-year return period storm (Scenario 1, Table 3.3-3). This is the most frequent mechanism in Delhi.

Key variables

Ceff — Effective drain capacity (Manning's n: 0.014 impervious, 0.012 RCC box, 0.025 open drain; Table 4.1-3). Reduced by Horton soil saturation factor (f∞ = 0.635 mm/hr for Group D loam; Table 4.3-1).
Rpeak — Peak 3-hour rainfall intensity (IDF-based; IIT Delhi Ch 4.4).
Trunoff — Terrain runoff multiplier from imperviousness (NCR Functional Drainage Plan Table 5.4-5.5).

Mechanism 2 — Fluvial (30%)

Fluvial = 100 − elevation_penalty − terrain_penalty − yamuna_discharge_penalty
River & outfall submergence

Yamuna overtops embankments or submerges drain outfalls. CWC 2023: stage 208.66 m flooded Civil Lines, Mayur Vihar, Patparganj entirely — different mechanism from rain. IIT Delhi DMP: “final disposal of majority of storm water is into Yamuna through outfall points” (Ch 1.2).

Key variables

Elevation — SRTM v4.1 ward mean elevation. Full penalty ≤207 m (Yamuna danger zone), zero ≥215 m, linear taper.
Terrain class — IIT Delhi basin delineation: floodplain (Trans-Yamuna alluvial clay), low, flat, ridge (Delhi Ridge/Aravalli).
Yamuna discharge — CWC Hathnikund gauge: Normal <100k cusecs, Danger >300k cusecs (48-72h travel time to Delhi).

Mechanism 3 — Compound (25%)

Compound = 100 − (rain_severity × river_severity × elevation_vulnerability × 80)
Simultaneous rain + high river (backflow)

The critical interaction: high Yamuna submerges drain outfalls, stormwater has nowhere to go. Even 20 mm/hr rain causes severe flooding. The July 2023 disaster was compound — heavy local rain AND Yamuna in flood simultaneously.

IIT Delhi basis

DMP Executive Summary: “storm water has to be managed so it does not interfere with drainage.” SWMM modelling shows junction flooding increases 3-5× when outfalls are submerged. Multiplicative interaction ensures this component only activates when BOTH rain AND river are elevated AND ward is low-lying.

Pluvial Mechanism: SWMM Conveyance Model
Drain capacity proxy (capacity_c) calibrated against IIT Delhi SWMM conduit-flow analysis. The ratio C/(R×T) maps to junction overflow volume under design storm. Najafgarh basin: 3,854 junctions flooded under 2-year storm (Scenario 1); reduced to 2,794 after cross-section correction (Scenario 2). Horton soil saturation (f∞=0.635 mm/hr, Group D loam) applied as multi-event capacity reduction factor.
Fluvial Mechanism: CWC Yamuna Thresholds
Central Water Commission, “Delhi Floods 2023 Case Study,” August 2024. Peak stage 208.66m at Old Railway Bridge (Jul 13), peak discharge 3,60,000 cusecs at Hathnikund (Jul 11). CWC warning level 204.5m, danger level 205.33m. Validated against INDOFLOODS gauge INDOFLOODS-gauge-164 (IIT Delhi HydroSense Lab, 2025, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.14584654).
Compound Mechanism: Drain Backflow
Interaction model grounded in IIT Delhi DMP Executive Summary: outfalls into Yamuna are final storm water disposal points; when river submerges outfalls, drain reversal occurs. DDMA documented 18 major drains experiencing reverse flow during Yamuna spate. Multiplicative interaction ensures compound score only activates when BOTH rain AND river are elevated AND ward is low-lying.
Basin Delineation & Catchment Validation
Delhi: 3 major basins delineated using SWAT (IIT Delhi Ch 1.2). Najafgarh (largest, ~2/3 Delhi area, 123 natural drains), Barapullah (44 drains), Trans-Yamuna (34 drains, former floodplain). Total: 201 natural drains + 3,311 km engineered storm drains. Catchment attributes cross-validated via INDOFLOODS (IIT Delhi HydroSense Lab, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.14584654).
Intervention Mapping
IIT Delhi DMP 5 scenarios map to interventions: (1) Base condition, (2) Cross-section correction → desilting; (3) Water body rejuvenation → detention basins; (4) Parks as recharge → permeable surfaces; (5) Storage/LID → rain gardens + permeable pavement. Nangloi case study: LID reduced J_3144 flood volume from 172,880 to 3,460 ×10³ litres (Table 3.3-8).
Three-mechanism model grounded in IIT Delhi DMP 2018 (SWMM/SWAT), CWC 2023 case study, and INDOFLOODS database. In production, direct PWD drain gauge integration would replace proxy capacity values.

Coverage

290 administrative wards covering MCD (Municipal Corporation of Delhi), NDMC (New Delhi Municipal Council), and Delhi Cantonment Board jurisdictions. Ward-level system extends IIT Delhi HydroSense Lab basin-level methodology to full city coverage.
